# Skills Quick Reference

*One-paragraph summaries. Full procedures in `Writing Projects/writing-skills.md`.*

---

## Skill 1: Expository Distillation
Reduce a draft to its logical core: definitions + propositions only. For expository files (explain, define, argue) — never apply to rhetorical, personal, narrative, or poetic drafts. Output format: `**Term** — one-sentence definition` plus bullet-point propositions, organized hierarchically. Proposition style: name an actor, use strong action verbs, avoid nominalizations (`attend` not `attention`). Handle metaphor by preserving it near its expository section as a blockquote, not by translating it.

---

## Skill 2: Expository Consolidation
Merge two Skill-1-formatted files with no redundancy and no loss. Compare definitions term-by-term; keep the more precise, more self-consistent, more Aquinas-trees-reference-consistent version. Combine complementary propositions. Re-order after merging into a single coherent argument. Decision rule: prefer more self-sufficient over more elegant.

---

## Skill 3: Rhetorical Revision
Sharpen a personal essay, prose poem, or meditation so the author's voice becomes more fully itself — not "improved writing in the abstract." Read without intervening first. Identify organizing logic, tonal register, and central image. Audit imagery: flag mixed metaphors (Thomistic vs. anthroposophical registers collide in this writing), decorative images, abstractions with no sensory anchor. Compress: cut adjective stacks, passive constructions, explanatory parentheses that tell the reader what to feel. Output: (1) Diagnostic, (2) Blueprint (3–5 targeted suggestions), (3) Proof (Before/After/What changed for one key passage).

---

## Skill 4: Rhetorical Consolidation
Given two drafts of the same piece, produce one master draft preserving the best of each. Identify fork points — genuine differences in metaphor, rhythm, level of abstraction, inclusion. Present side-by-side table for each fork with a one-sentence reason for the recommendation. Salvage outtakes: beautiful lines that don't fit go in `## Outtakes` at the end, labeled by source. Decision rule: concrete image over abstract elaboration; one word over two; strange/unexpected phrasing over conventionally good; register established at the opening.

---

## Skill 5: Vectorial Analysis — Expository
Before consolidating expository files, decompose into typed atomic elements: **D** (Definition), **SP** (Simple Proposition — one actor, one action, one consequence), **CP** (Complex Proposition — relates two or more concepts). Label every sentence; split sentences that do two things. Lay matching elements side by side. For each group: identify redundancy, complementarity, contradiction, or unique content. Resolve contradictions explicitly. Reassemble: definitions first, then SP, then CP.

---

## Skill 6: Vectorial Analysis — Rhetorical
Before consolidating rhetorical drafts, decompose into functional modes: **Im** (Image/metaphor), **De** (Description), **Na** (Narrative), **Mu** (Musing/reflection), **Pr** (Process), **CC** (Compare/contrast), **Cl** (Claim). Map the mode-sequence of each draft. Compare mode-by-mode: which has the stronger Im for the same moment? Identify missing modes: all Mu with no Im has no anchor; all De with no Cl has no interiority. Decision rule: stronger sensory anchor beats musing when both cover the same ground; opens outward rather than explaining itself.

---

## Skill 8: Unified Vectorial Analysis
The skill for when expository and rhetorical material cannot be cleanly separated, or when two contending frameworks (Aquinas + Steiner, etc.) must be consolidated piecemeal. Decomposes text into all element types simultaneously — **D** (definition), **SP** (simple proposition), **CP** (complex proposition), **Im** (rhetorical/poetic element) — and adds an **In** (intent/consequence) tag to every element stating what it is *doing* and what would be lost if cut. When working across two texts, labels each element by source framework (`[A]`/`[T]`/`[O]`) and lays matching elements side by side. Conflicts must be named and decided, not silently resolved. Reassembles in order (D → SP → CP, with Im elements placed where their In function performs), then checks for register consistency at expository-to-rhetorical transitions. Outtakes from any cut element with a live In tag go to `## Outtakes` — nothing with a live function is permanently deleted.

---

## Skill 9: Five-Pass Structural Rewrite
For raw or chaotic drafts that have real ideas but no clear argumentative structure. Runs five sequential passes: (1) content outline — extract propositions grouped by what they're about; (2) functional outline — reorganize the same propositions by what job they do in the argument, and explain why the functional grouping is more useful; (3) refine for flow — add topic sentences and transitions, flag seams; (4) rewrite — use the refined outline as skeleton while preserving the original's strongest images, phrases, and rhetorical moves; (5) revise — reread for stalled flow and for implications the draft didn't follow through on, letting genuine instabilities stay open rather than papering over them. Run all five passes before asking for direction unless the source material's scope is ambiguous.

---

## Skill 10: Rhetorical Realization
Take a section cleaned by Skill 1 and rewrite it into the piece's rhetorical register. Apply only after Skill 1 — never before (rhetorical realization on unstable content wastes the work). For each definition, find the image that embodies it; for each proposition, show it in operation rather than stating it. Do not over-explain: if the image makes the point, cut the summary sentence. Output: a rewritten passage in the piece's voice, plus a note on any proposition that resisted realization. Register reference for Zine work: the opening oak scene and the after-image passage in master.md.

---

## Skill 11: Seed Development
Take a cluster of seeds (bullet notes, fragments, aphorisms, single lines) and grow them into a first rhetorical draft. Step 1: name the beat's question in one sentence. Step 2: inventory seeds by type — Im (image), Cl (claim), Q (open question), Ph (philosophical source), Na (narrative). Step 3: find the opening — must be Im or Na, mid-scene, withholding. Step 4: sequence seeds (opening → claims → philosophical depth → closing Im or Q). Step 5: draft in the piece's register — second person, short paragraphs, no over-explanation. Step 6: place resistant seeds (no image anchor, register mismatch) in `## Unplaced`. Output: first draft + Unplaced seeds. Register reference: Beat 1–2 master.md opening; Kali Yuga passage in tree-archetypes-angels-kali-yuga.md.

---

## Skill 12: Non-Technical Audience Translation
Take a finished piece and adapt it for a reader with no domain background — without changing the core claim. Step 1: model the new reader in one sentence (who they are, what they bring). Step 2: flag every term or concept that requires prior domain knowledge. Step 3: build a dependency chain — what must be grounded first before other flags can be resolved. Step 4: for each flagged item, introduce an everyday analogy or concrete example before naming the term. Step 5: check for logic gaps — each sentence followable from the previous without a gap. Step 6: strip residual domain register artifacts. Step 7: verify the claim survived — simplification that changes the claim is a failure. Output: translated piece + fidelity note stating what was preserved, simplified, or bracketed.

---

## Skill 13: Clarity Loop Revision
Systematically eliminate explanation debt — places where the text assumes understanding it has not yet given the reader. Iterates until no debt remains. Pass 1: state what the reader knows on page one. Pass 2: underline every new concept or logical move the reader could not have derived from what came before. Pass 3: audit — is each underlined item grounded before it appears? Pass 4: pay each debt with the minimum grounding needed (one sentence, one instance, one bridging clause). Pass 5: loop — new grounding may create new debt; re-read from the beginning and repeat Passes 2–4 until clean. Pass 6: spine check — read only topic sentences in sequence; do they form a coherent path without the body text? Pass 7: verify the piece still moves — tighten grounding language if it has become labored. Common debt patterns: jargon-as-shorthand, implicit contrast, dropped "therefore," circular grounding.

---

## Skill 7: Sequence and Assembly — Long Work/Poem
Determine final order, opening move, and transitions for a long-form piece. Step 1: Inventory every beat and its status — do not begin assembly until all beats are draft-ready. Step 2: Choose the opening (must use Im or De; must arrive mid-scene without explanation; must withhold enough to open a question). Step 3: Map transitions as imagistic rhymes — last image of beat N echoes or inverts first image of beat N+1 (prose connectives almost always wrong). Step 4: Place structural detours where the reader needs a breath, not where they thematically belong. Step 6: Read aloud — this is the final test.
