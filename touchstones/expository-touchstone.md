# Expository Touchstone

*The quality standard for expository/reference prose in this project.*

The canonical example is `Writing Projects/Zine/_rhetorical/02b-perception-form-trees/reference.md`.

---

## What makes it the touchstone

**Every line either defines or proposes.** There is no prose padding, no transition filler, no restatement of what the heading already said. Each sentence advances the argument.

**Hierarchical organization with no redundancy.** Parent concepts with nested children. The same term does not appear in two sections with two different definitions — or if it does, one section owns it and the other points to the owner.

**Definitions have genus + differentia.** Each term is placed in a category (`what kind of thing it is`) and distinguished within that category (`what makes it this rather than something else in the category`).

**Propositions name an actor and use strong action verbs.** `Form organizes matter` not `organization is provided by form`; `esse enters through counterspace` not `counterspace is related to esse`.

**Poetic material is preserved, not translated.** When a poetic phrase is necessary — the kind that compresses an argument into an image — it appears once, in a clearly marked section or as a blockquote near its expository equivalent. It is never paraphrased into exposition; it is kept intact or cut.

---

## What to check against it

When revising an expository draft, ask:
- Does every sentence define or propose? Cut any sentence that only restates, transitions, or describes the document.
- Is each term defined exactly once, in a section strong enough to own it?
- Do the definitions have genus + differentia, or are they functional descriptions masquerading as definitions?
- Are there nominalizations that could be verbed?
- Is any poetic phrase repeated? It should appear exactly once.
