# Decision Rules

*Standing rules extracted from revision work, skills definitions, and editorial notes. Add to this file whenever a new rule emerges from the Reflect phase.*

---

## For all writing

**Concrete image over abstract elaboration.** When two versions cover the same ground — one grounded in sensation, one in abstraction — keep the concrete. The abstraction can be rebuilt from the image; the reverse is rarely true.

**One word over two.** When a phrase can be cut to a single word without loss, cut it.

**Strange over conventionally good.** Unexpected phrasing that is precise beats elegant phrasing that is predictable.

**The register established at the opening governs the whole piece.** Shifts in register (from intimate to clinical, from liturgical to analytical) need to be intentional and earned, not incidental.

**Read aloud before calling anything final.** The test for this writing is sound. Pace collapse, false transitions, and premature peaks are audible before they're visible.

---

## For expository writing

**Accuracy before synthesis.** Describe each tradition accurately on its own terms first. Then flag cross-cultural or cross-tradition synthesis explicitly with a transition sentence. Never silently blend.

**Name an actor. Use strong action verbs. Avoid nominalizations.** `attend` not `attention`; `perceive` not `perception`; `the form organizes matter` not `organization of matter by form`.

**Every definition requires genus + differentia.** What category does this belong to, and what distinguishes it within that category? Functional descriptions and formal definitions can coexist if both are labeled.

**A poetic phrase loses its power through repetition.** If a phrase appears in the poetic phrase map, it should appear exactly once in the document.

**Prefer more self-sufficient over more elegant.** When two versions of a definition are both accurate, keep the one that doesn't require the reader to hold another definition in mind to understand it.

**Unique content goes to `## Come back to`, not the trash.** When a proposition doesn't fit the current section's argument, preserve it with a destination note rather than deleting it.

---

## For rhetorical / essay writing

**Do not cut dense lines merely because they're difficult.** Difficulty that earns its difficulty is not the same as obscurity. Cut explanatory parentheses that tell the reader what to feel; do not cut lines that require the reader to think.

**Decorative images get cut; images carrying argumentative or emotional weight stay.** The test: if the image were removed, would the reader lose something they couldn't recover from the surrounding prose?

**The strongest single adjective beats a stack.** Adjective stacks (`dark, heavy, ancient`) almost always mean none of them is right. Find the one that does the work alone.

**Chronic failure mode: losing the concrete anchor while elaborating the abstraction.** In this writing, the spiritual/philosophical register tends to expand and crowd out the phenomenological detail that grounds it. Audit for this specifically.

**Chronic failure mode: over-explaining the spiritual register at the expense of the image.** The image can hold philosophical weight without being told what weight it's holding.

---

## For consolidating two drafts

**Identify fork points, not just word changes.** A fork is a genuine difference in metaphor, rhythm, level of abstraction, or what's included. Minor word variation is not a fork.

**Outtakes are not waste.** Beautiful lines that don't fit the master draft go in `## Outtakes` labeled by source draft. Nothing valuable is permanently deleted in a consolidation pass.

**The owning section for a definition must be strong enough to own it.** Before consolidating a term into its owning section, check whether that section needs enrichment first. Consolidation into a weak section produces a weaker document.

---

## For long-form assembly

**Begin in the body, not the mind.** Opening beats must use Im or De mode. Mu (musing/reflection) as an opening almost always fails.

**Arrive mid-scene without explanation.** Withhold context. Open a question the rest of the piece pursues.

**Imagistic rhymes, not prose connectives.** The best transitions are when the last image of one beat echoes or inverts the first image of the next. `And so` / `From here` / `This brings us to` — almost always wrong.

**Place structural detours where the reader needs a breath, not where they thematically belong.** Thematic logic is not the same as reading logic.

---

## For sourcing and research

**Distinguish well-documented from partially documented facts.** When uncertain, note it at the point of the claim: `(verify before treating as established)`. The oldtime-history project models this.

**Research-for-writing requires conceptual preparation, not just factual collection.** The david-need-prep.md is the model: biographical background, bibliographic depth, conceptual cross-reference to your own project, and prepared questions organized by topic before the encounter that will become writing material.
